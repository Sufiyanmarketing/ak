For 3,000 years, we've been told the Greeks took Troy with an army hidden inside a wooden horse. But the horse held no army. Just a few dozen men. The fleet had only sailed behind a nearby island — and the Trojans hauled the horse in themselves. That night, the men slid down ropes, opened the gate from inside, and lit a torch for the ships. So Troy didn't fall to a horse. It fell to one silent night, and a gate opened from within.

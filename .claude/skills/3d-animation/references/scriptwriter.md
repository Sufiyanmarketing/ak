# Scriptwriting — the curiosity loop

The channel's real growth engine is not animation — it is a script that opens a
loop in the viewer's brain that they physically need closed. Every script is one
loop: the first line raises the question, the last line closes it with a reframe.

## The 5-beat structure (default 28–32s ≈ 75–85 words)

| Beat | Share | Job | Pattern |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. MYTH | ~30% | state the story everyone believes, as fact, with a time-scale hook | "For 3,000 years, we've been told…" |
| 2. TWIST | ~12% | snap the rug: authority + negation | "But historians now think no such X existed." |
| 3. MECHANISM A | ~22% | the first real explanation — physical, visual, specific | "To break down gates, armies used…" |
| 4. MECHANISM B | ~22% | the second explanation — stranger, bigger | "Others think the X was actually…" |
| 5. KICKER | ~14% | close the loop by reframing beat 1 with both mechanisms in one image | "So X may have fallen to …, or to …" |

Rate ≈ 2.7 words/second. Beat boundaries become the video's section boundaries.

## Reference script (the Trojan Horse short — imitate this shape)

> For 3,000 years, we've been told the Greeks hid inside a giant wooden horse
> to take Troy. **(MYTH, 0–5.8s)**
> But historians now think no such horse existed. **(TWIST, 5.8–9.2s)**
> To break down gates, armies used battering rams covered in wet horse skin so
> it wouldn't burn. **(MECHANISM A, 9.2–15.6s)**
> Others think the horse was an earthquake — because Poseidon ruled the sea,
> horses, and earthquakes. **(MECHANISM B, 15.6–22.1s)**
> So Troy may have fallen to a log dressed as a horse, or to the ground
> itself. **(KICKER, 22.1–28.6s)**

Why it works: the twist attacks a story the viewer has known since childhood;
both mechanisms are *filmable physics* (wet hide vs torch, crack racing through
a wall); the kicker is a double image that resolves the title question.

## Writing rules

- Every sentence must be **filmable** — if you can't storyboard it in 3 shots,
  rewrite it. "Armies used battering rams covered in wet horse skin" is
  filmable; "siege warfare evolved" is not.
- Numbers beat adjectives: "3,000 years", "50 people" — one number in beat 1.
- Authority phrases carry the twist: "historians now think", "engineers found",
  "footage shows".
- No greetings, no meta, no throat-clearing, no "let's find out". Cold open.
- Facts must be defensible: research the topic (2+ sources) before writing;
  keep a Sources line in the working notes (never in the video).
- Mechanisms must be TRUE claims someone actually holds — this is myth-busting,
  not fiction. "Others think" is the honesty valve.

## Rewrite pass (run before showing the script)

1. Read it aloud at 2.7 w/s — over budget? Cut hedges ("some", "perhaps",
   "it is believed that") first, then merge clauses.
2. Replace every abstract noun with a physical one (deception → wet horse skin).
3. Check the loop: does the last line answer the exact question the first line
   raised? If not, rewrite the kicker, not the hook.
4. Check faction clarity: can a viewer track WHO does WHAT with zero names
   beyond the famous ones? (Use "the Greeks", "armies", "others" — never
   character names.)
